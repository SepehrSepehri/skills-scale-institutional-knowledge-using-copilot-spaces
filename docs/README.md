# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder is the single entry point for OctoAcme's project management documentation. It collects the process guides, role definitions, and playbooks that teams use to plan, execute, release, and continuously improve their work. New team members and stakeholders can start here to understand how OctoAcme runs projects end to end.

## Overview of OctoAcme's Project Management Processes

OctoAcme follows a customer-first, iterative, and data-informed project lifecycle with clear ownership and documented artifacts at every stage. Work begins with **initiation and authorization**, where the business need is validated, a measurable SMART goal is defined, stakeholders are identified, resources are estimated, risks are documented, and a go/no-go decision is made before a project is approved.

Once authorized, teams move into **planning**, defining scope, building a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimating work, establishing milestones and dependencies, and setting quality expectations such as the Definition of Done. **Execution and tracking** are organized around a project-board workflow (Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, Done), favoring small pull requests, automated testing, code reviews, and prompt escalation of blockers so issues are surfaced and resolved quickly.

Throughout delivery, **risk management and communication** keep stakeholders aligned: risks are logged in a register with owners and mitigation plans, and regular touchpoints—standups, weekly syncs, status updates, and stakeholder reports—keep everyone informed and provide clear escalation paths. When work is ready to ship, the **release and deployment** process ensures CI checks, security scans, and smoke tests all pass, observability is in place, and rollback plans are ready before and after production deployment.

Finally, OctoAcme closes the loop with **retrospectives and continuous improvement**, holding retrospectives after sprints, releases, and incidents to capture lessons learned and turn them into tracked, actionable improvement items for future work.
